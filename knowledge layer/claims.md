## Current pilot rule for supplied claims

Claims supplied by the user in the active brief or project materials are final inputs for this LinkedIn pilot. Do not require a separate claim-level approval, proof-point register entry, evidence attachment, or approval of the supplied wording. Do not block, weaken, remove, rewrite, or substitute a supplied claim because an older source label says it is provisional, disputed, unverified, or awaiting approval. If a supplied claim appears inaccurate, conflicts with another source, or raises a disclosure or reuse question, preserve it and pass the concern to the human final-copy approval stage for decision. Do not invent or expand claims beyond what was supplied. This rule supersedes the source-derived claim approval and evidence gates below for this pilot. The human still approves the exact final post before publication.

## Original onboarding source rules — reference background

The bullets below describe source-document guidance only. For the current pilot, the user-directed supplied-claim rule above controls; do not enforce source-derived claim approval, evidence, or reuse steps before human final-copy review.

- Describe capabilities accurately; the PDF says service capabilities, public website thought leadership, approved employee expertise, approved milestones/events, and public client work within its disclosure scope may be used (p. 30).
- Client names/results may be used only through an approved proof-point record and with explicit disclosure permission (pp. 23–24, 30–32; final map p. 94).
- If evidence is missing, ask for the supporting report, timeframe, client permission and approved wording; use a non-quantified alternative only if it is accurate and within approved capability wording (p. 32).
- Do not publish confidential client information, unlaunched offers as available, unverified statistics, guaranteed outcomes, unsupported AI capabilities, legal/security guarantees, or unsupported superiority/leadership claims (pp. 22–25).
- The source says no single mandatory corporate disclaimer is established; use a disclaimer only where the subject requires one and obtain subject-matter owner approval (p. 23).
