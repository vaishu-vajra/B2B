## Item 1 — From AI experiments to connected marketing workflows

### Post copy

Adding an AI tool is easy. Connecting it to the way marketing work gets done is the harder question.

An isolated experiment can show what a tool might do. A connected workflow asks how it fits with the people, processes and decisions around it—and whether the work can be evaluated against a meaningful outcome.

For marketing leaders, the next step may be to map one process from input to action, then identify where AI belongs and what should still involve human judgment. That makes the conversation more practical than starting with a list of tools.

## Item 2 — Designing connected AI-enabled marketing workflows

### Post copy

AI-enabled marketing is not a tool decision alone. It is a design decision about how strategy, technology, processes and execution fit together.

If those parts are disconnected, teams can end up with a new platform sitting beside existing work instead of supporting it. A useful starting point is to choose one marketing workflow, clarify the outcome it serves, and trace how information and decisions move through it.

That gives leaders a clearer basis for deciding where AI could help, what needs to connect, and where people should stay involved.

## Item 5 — A practical B2B marketing point of view

### Post copy

Before choosing a content format, name the buyer decision the content should help someone make.

A format is a way to deliver a message. It does not decide what the reader needs to understand, compare or act on. Starting with the buyer decision gives the team a clearer test: does this content help the reader move through that decision, or is it simply filling a channel?

That question can sharpen the brief before anyone chooses a post, article or other format. It also keeps the work anchored in buyer intent and content alignment.
