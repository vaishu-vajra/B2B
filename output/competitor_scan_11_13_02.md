## 3. Industry developments

- **AI maturity and integration:** EY’s *Marketing in the AI Era: From Campaigns to Connected Intelligence*, produced with MMA, reports a survey of 60+ marketers across 11 sectors in India, surveyed in February 2026. Its findings describe uneven integration: 65% of respondents using AI in content/creative relied on standalone tools, while 35% reported full-stack use cases; in measurement and insights, the report shows 43% full-stack and 57% standalone. The report also says about 84% of marketers achieved medium or high impact from AI integration in measurement and insights. Source: [EY report PDF](https://www.ey.com/content/dam/ey-unified-site/ey-com/en-in/insights/ai/documents/ey-marketing-in-the-ai-era-from-campaigns-to-connected-intelligence.pdf); the study is also covered in [Exchange4Media, 5 Nov 2026](https://www.exchange4media.com/digital-news/ai-adoption-in-indian-marketing-rises-but-full-scale-integration-remains-a-challenge-ey-159917.html). **Grade C** — primary report and dated study; the respondent group is limited and survey responses are not independently verified. The report publication date is not stated on the PDF itself.
- **Potential competitive impact:** The study points to workflow integration, decision quality, measurement and human judgment as active issues alongside AI adoption. This supports the relevance of connected-workflow and human-checkpoint themes, but does not establish demand for Vajra services or prove a Vajra capability.

## 4. Competitive analysis

### Observations
- Social Beat’s dated public material describes connected media planning, measurement and AI creative production (PRISM, 12 Oct), creator intelligence (Influencer.in, 6 Oct), and AI visibility measurement (GEO Pulse, 18 Sep; rechecked 13 Nov).
- Schbang’s 3 Nov roundup showcases campaign-led work across brand identity, seasonal films, creator activity, social-first campaigns and AI-led creative. Its current LinkedIn snapshot includes an LLM visibility case claim and November content planning.
- Performics India’s public material combines performance/media measurement with AI agent learning and team capability-building. Recent posts include the “25th Hour” program and an AI-agent workshop, with relative timestamps.
- ibs Fulcro's official website describes integrated digital marketing and technology services for enterprise brands; its public LinkedIn snapshot was incomplete and did not support a frequency estimate.
- YAAP Digital's FY 2025–26 annual report describes integrated digital marketing, content and technology services across several sectors and geographies. The public LinkedIn feed was unavailable in this run.
- The EY-MMA survey report describes substantial use of standalone AI tools alongside emerging full-stack use across marketing functions.
- None of the observed sources provides a complete, reliably dated LinkedIn history or a consistent, comparable engagement dataset for all three competitors.

### Interpretations
- In this sample, the competitors show AI through distinct proof routes: Social Beat through connected advertising/creative and measurement products; Schbang through integrated campaign work and search/LLM visibility examples; Performics India through performance systems and internal AI enablement.
- Across the sampled material, competitors connect AI to workflows, measurement, team capability or discovery rather than presenting it only as a generic tool. This is an interpretation of a limited public-source sample, not a claim about the whole market.
- The industry study reinforces a gap between tool use and cross-functional integration. This is a report-based interpretation, not proof of a specific customer need or a forecast of market demand.
- The discovered candidates both present combined marketing and technology capabilities, though their public service claims do not establish that they are Vajra competitors. ibs Fulcro is the closer fit to the enterprise technology/marketing scope reviewed; YAAP is more broadly consumer-marketing oriented.

### Hypotheses

1. **Potential content angle — workflow ownership and human checkpoints.** A useful general thought-leadership angle could show how a team connects one AI task to inputs, a review decision and a next handoff. This aligns with Vajra’s internally recorded positioning around connecting strategy, technology, creativity and execution (brand v0.4). **Required evidence before a Vajra-specific claim:** approved current service wording, a documented Vajra example, supporting evidence for any outcome, and any applicable client permission from claims v0.2. **Status: Unvalidated; keep as general advice unless those records are approved.**
2. **Potential content angle — measurement before claiming AI impact.** Discuss how teams define a measurement frame before describing AI-enabled business results. This is compatible with the internal growth/marketing positioning, but no Vajra-specific measurement capability or outcome is verified by this scan. **Required evidence:** approved capability statement and an evidence-backed result with timeframe, metric definition and reuse permission. **Status: Unvalidated.**
